## Breakdowns (paraphrased)

1. **Fix your entire life.** New Year moment. Opens on why resolutions fail, then promises a full reset in one day. Long how-to with numbered parts and an exact day protocol. Bookmarks far above likes.
2. **Something Big Is Happening.** Opens with a shared-memory analogy to early 2020, then the author's years in the field, then "this is for the people I care about." Pure text, no images, written to be forwarded to non-tech friends.
3. **SpaceX & the Sentient Sun.** Opens on a public pay-package target, then a track record of predictions, then a science-fiction frame. Eight claim headers that build from first principles to a far future. Heavy use of charts. Ends with a disclosure.
4. **Deloitte exposé.** "You were not supposed to see this" frame, a big number in the title, proof of work from contract data, then a rapid list of cases. Short and dense.
5. **Slavery history.** Personal anecdote opener, then "I decided to investigate. What I found was worse." Roman-numeral sections with source quotes. Close: an aphorism, a share ask, and a sources link.
6. **Tariff playbook.** "The trade war is back. We spent 12 months analyzing every move. Bookmark this." Numbered steps ending in "repeat from step 1". Close: one line plus a follow ask.
7. **Multiple interests.** Names a shame the reader feels, then the author's own struggle, then "this is the best time to be alive" and a count of ideas. Roman numerals I to VII with path and step subheads. Signs off by first name.

## What separates the top tier

- **Audience.** 15M+ stakes are universal (life, job, money, country). The 1M to 15M tier often serves one group well (traders, founders, a storm region).
- **Title.** Top titles promise a total outcome or a large hidden truth, often with a number or a blunt warning. Mid-tier titles more often describe a topic.
- **First 300 words.** Top pieces name a private feeling, prove effort, and state the payoff before the first header. Mid-tier openings more often start with information or background.
- **Emotional promise.** Transformation, alarm, or vindication rather than "useful info".
- **Timing.** Most top pieces landed on a moment the whole feed was already feeling, or in the contest window.
- **Format.** Either very long (4,800 to 7,200 words) with numbered parts, or short and dense (about 1,000 to 1,200 words) with hard facts and dated steps.
- **Promotion.** All 7 were shared from the author's account as the article link alone. These are large accounts, so a smaller account should add a line of tension.
- **Engagement shape.** How-tos earn bookmarks above likes. Provocations earn reposts and replies.
